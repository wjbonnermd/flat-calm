Our Story

The water could be rough, but my dad always called it flat calm.

Wind kicking up whitecaps, chop slapping the hull, spray coming over the bow. He'd look out at all of it, smile, and say "flat calm." It wasn't a weather report. It was an outlook: we're out here, the day is good, and the conditions are whatever you make of them.

That's what this brand is about. We make gear and apparel for people who see the good side of every day on the water, choppy or not. It's built to handle salt and sun, and it's comfortable enough that you'll keep wearing it long after you've hosed off the boat.

Weather or not, it's always flat calm.

Field Guide

The Flat Calm scale

Sailors read the sea with the Beaufort scale, which runs from force 0 (calm) to force 12 (hurricane). Here are the first seven steps, next to Dad's reading of each one.

ForceWindWhat you'll seeWhat Dad called it

  1. Force 0

    Under 1 kt

    Glass

    The sea is a mirror. Every cloud shows up twice.

    Flat calm.

  2. Force 1

    1–3 kt

    Ripples

    Scaly ripples, no foam on the crests.

    Flat calm.

  3. Force 2

    4–6 kt

    Wavelets

    Small, short wavelets. The crests look glassy and don't break.

    Flat calm.

  4. Force 3

    7–10 kt

    Light chop

    Larger wavelets. Crests start to break, with a few scattered whitecaps.

    Flat calm.

  5. Force 4

    11–16 kt

    Chop

    Small waves getting longer. Whitecaps are fairly common.

    Flat calm.

  6. Force 5

    17–21 kt

    Whitecaps

    Moderate waves, whitecaps everywhere, some spray over the bow.

    Flat calm.

  7. Force 6

    22–27 kt

    Big water

    Large waves, white foam crests all around, plenty of spray.

    Still flat calm. We'll fish the bay.

Flat calm is an outlook, not a forecast. Check the marine forecast before you head out, and know when to stay at the dock.

Fishing

Five things we've learned fishing the dawn slick

Why the first hour after light is worth the alarm clock.

Read the story
  1. The wind usually isn't up yet. On a lot of coasts the sea breeze doesn't fill in until the sun has warmed the land, so first light is often the calmest water of the day.
  2. Bait sits high in low light. Watch for nervous water and diving birds. Fish push bait to the surface early, and you can see it from a long way off on a slick.
  3. Fish the tide, not just the clock. Moving water at dawn beats slack water at dawn. Check the tide times the night before and plan around them.
  4. Start on top. Flat water and soft light are when topwater lures shine. Go deeper as the sun climbs.
  5. Rig the night before. Every knot you tie at the ramp is a minute of the best light you don't get back.

Coast

A perfect beach day, packed into one cooler

Our list for a full day on the sand, from shade to snacks.

Read the story
  • Pre-chill it. Put a bag of ice in the cooler the night before, then dump it in the morning. A warm cooler burns through ice fast.
  • Block ice beats cubes. It lasts much longer. Frozen water bottles work too, and you can drink them in the afternoon.
  • Pack in reverse. What you'll eat last goes on the bottom, and lunch and snacks go on top.
  • Drinks get their own cooler. The food cooler stays shut and stays cold.
  • Keep it in the shade, with a towel over the lid.

Outside the cooler: shade, sunscreen, a towel big enough for two, and a trash bag for the walk out.

Conservation

Leave it better: the one-bag beach cleanup

An easy habit for every beach day. No event or sign-up needed.

Read the story

Bring one bag, an old mesh dive bag or a bucket, and fill it on the walk back to the car. That's it. Ten minutes, every trip.

  • Wear gloves and watch for glass, hooks, and anything sharp.
  • Leave the natural stuff. The line of seaweed and shells at the high-tide mark is food and shelter for shorebirds. Take only what people left.
  • Grab the fishing line first. Tangled line is one of the worst things on a beach for birds and turtles. Many piers and boat ramps have monofilament recycling bins for it.

Pack out what you brought, plus one thing you didn't.
